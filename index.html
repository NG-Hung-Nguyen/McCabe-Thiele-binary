Directions

The McCabe-Thiele method finds the number of equilibrium stages a binary column needs, with nothing more than the x–y equilibrium curve and two straight mass-balance lines. Work through the six steps with the Step buttons above the diagram, or press Next.

The six steps

  1. Equilibrium curve. Plot y* against x for the light (more volatile) component at the column pressure, together with the diagonal y = x.
  2. Compositions. Mark xB, zF and xD on the diagonal.
  3. Feed line. Draw the q-line from (zF, zF) with slope q/(q−1).
  4. Rectifying line. Draw the rectifying line from (xD, xD) to the y-axis intercept xD/(R+1).
  5. Stripping line. Join (xB, xB) to the point where the rectifying line meets the q-line.
  6. Stages. Step off stages from the top: horizontally to the equilibrium curve, then vertically to the operating line, until x ≤ xB.

Use the diagram

  • Drag the dots: the three dots on the diagonal set the compositions, the filled amber dot turns the q-line, and the filled blue dot on the y-axis sets the reflux ratio. Or type values in the boxes above. Click a dot (or Tab to it) and use the arrow keys; hold Shift for larger steps.
  • In step 6, add stages one at a time with + Add stage, or show them all. Count them yourself first: choose Hide answers to blur the computed results.
  • View zooms into the top or bottom end of the diagram, where high-purity stages get very small.
  • Feed stage: Fixed lets you put the feed on any stage (for example the stage used in your Aspen model) and see what happens when it is not the optimal one.
  • Murphree efficiency below 1 draws a dashed pseudo-equilibrium curve and steps to it instead of the true curve, which gives real trays instead of equilibrium stages.

Load your own binary system from Aspen Plus

  1. In Aspen Plus, run a Binary analysis (Home tab, Analysis group) of type Txy at your column pressure. Several pressures in one run are fine; you can switch between them here.
  2. Open the results, select the whole results table including the header row (PRES, MOLEFRAC …, TOTAL TEMP, VAPOR MOLEFRAC …) and the units row, and copy it.
  3. Either click Paste table and paste it directly, or paste it into an Excel sheet, save it as .xlsx and click Load .xlsx / .csv. The same workbook you use for the Binary VLE Diagrams page works here.
  4. Data from other sources also works: a .csv or pasted table with two columns x, y (and optionally a third column T), with x and y the mole fractions of the same component.

Details

Assumptions of the method

  • Constant molar overflow (CMO): the liquid and vapor molar flows are constant within each section. This holds when both components have similar molar heats of vaporization and heat losses and heats of mixing are small. That is why the operating lines are straight.
  • Constant column pressure: one x–y curve is used for the whole column.
  • Each stage is an equilibrium stage (unless you set a Murphree efficiency below 1). The partial reboiler counts as an equilibrium stage. A total condenser does not; a partial condenser does.

How the page draws the equilibrium curve

No thermodynamic model runs here. The y–x points from the data are joined by a monotone cubic (PCHIP) interpolation, which passes through every data point and adds no wiggles. With Constant α the curve is y = αx / (1 + (α−1)x).

Feed stage

With Optimal, the page switches from the rectifying to the stripping line on the first stage whose liquid xn lies below the intersection of the two operating lines. That stage is the feed stage, and it gives the fewest stages. With Fixed, the switch happens on the stage you choose. If the feed is too high or too low, the steps pinch against the equilibrium curve and the column may never reach xB.

Minimum reflux and minimum stages

Rmin is the lowest reflux ratio at which both operating lines still lie on or below the equilibrium curve everywhere between xB and xD. The page finds it by bisection, so it also catches a tangent pinch away from the feed (for example ethanol–water). Nmin is stepped off between the curve and the diagonal (total reflux), and it is also given by the Fenske equation with the geometric-mean α. The Gilliland correlation (Molokanov form) gives a shortcut estimate of N from R, Rmin and Nmin, the same method Aspen's DSTWU block uses.

Stage numbers here and in Aspen Plus

Here, stages are counted from the top, and stage 1 is the top equilibrium stage. Aspen RadFrac counts the condenser as stage 1 and the reboiler as the last stage. With a total condenser, McCabe-Thiele stage n is RadFrac stage n + 1, so NSTAGE = N + 1. With a partial condenser the condenser is already McCabe-Thiele stage 1, so NSTAGE = N.

Why the result differs from RadFrac

RadFrac solves full energy balances on every stage, so its flows change from stage to stage; McCabe-Thiele assumes CMO. For methanol–water, the heat of vaporization of water (about 40.7 kJ/mol at its boiling point) is higher than that of methanol (about 35.3 kJ/mol), so the vapor flow in the real column changes along the column and the two methods give somewhat different stage counts.

About

A companion to the Binary VLE Diagrams page, built for a course that uses Aspen Plus for distillation design. It shows the hand calculation behind a column model, so the simulator is less of a black box.

The step-by-step construction follows the idea of the Construct a McCabe-Thiele Diagram for Distillation Wolfram Demonstration (LearnChemE, University of Colorado Boulder).

The page is a single self-contained HTML file. Equations are typeset with KaTeX; the spreadsheet reader (SheetJS) loads only when you open an .xlsx file. Nothing you load leaves your browser.
